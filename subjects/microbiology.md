# Microbiology

**Exam date:** 5 December 2026  
**NEP-2023 code:** BTBTC304 · **Archived CBCS code:** BTBTC08

> **Syllabus caution:** Topic map based on the archived NSUT CBCS Biotechnology scheme (2019–20). Check current IMS/faculty notes for the definitive 2026 content.

## Topic map

- **History and contributions:** Leeuwenhoek, Pasteur, Koch and related milestones.
- **Methods:** sterilisation, pure-culture isolation, enrichment culture, maintenance and preservation.
- **Prokaryotic anatomy:** cell envelope, wall, membrane, capsule, appendages, cytoplasm, inclusions and spores.
- **Microbial diversity:** Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya (algae, fungi, slime molds and protozoa), viruses, viroids and prions.
- **Nutrition and growth:** culture media, growth physiology and growth factors.
- **Metabolism:** ATP, reducing power, precursor metabolites, autotrophy/heterotrophy, aerobic/anaerobic ATP generation, nitrogen/sulfur assimilation and biosynthesis.
- Microbial ecology and applications in medicine, food, agriculture and energy.

## Questions visible in that sample paper

- Spontaneous generation theory and the origin of the idea.
- Isolating and confirming a pure culture.
- Germ theory and discovery of microorganisms.
- Growth factors and why they are required.
- Enrichment method and a workflow for isolating thermophilic bacteria.
- Oxygen requirements in microbial growth; Appertization vs Tyndallization; agar and its significance.

## Additional self-test prompts — not PYQs

1. Design a pure-culture isolation workflow and explain how purity is confirmed.
2. Compare sterilisation approaches; explain the principle and sequence of Tyndallization and Appertization.
3. Design an enrichment workflow for thermophilic bacteria; state selective conditions and expected observations.
4. Explain the microbial growth curve and effects of temperature, pH, oxygen and nutrients.
5. Compare Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya; distinguish cells from viruses, viroids and prions.
6. Explain ATP, reducing power and precursor metabolites in biosynthesis; compare phototrophic and chemotrophic energy generation.

## Answer-writing method

For a method question, write **purpose → principle → steps/workflow → controls or confirmation → result**. For comparisons, use a table. For organisms and cell structures, draw and label.
