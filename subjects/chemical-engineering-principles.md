# Chemical Engineering Principles

**Exam date:** 3 December 2026  
**NEP-2023 code:** BTBTC303 · **Archived CBCS code:** BTBTC07

> **Syllabus caution:** This map is based on the archived NSUT CBCS Biotechnology scheme (2019–20). The current IMS/faculty syllabus and taught examples take precedence.

## Topic map

- **Engineering calculations:** variables, dimensions, unit conversions, dimensional homogeneity and stoichiometry.
- **Material and energy balances:** general mass-balance equation, modular/continuous processes, reactive systems, enthalpy changes, steam tables, heat of reaction and microbial-process energy balances.
- **Reaction engineering:** rate, order and molecularity; kinetic interpretation of batch-reactor data; CSTR and plug-flow reactor design equations.
- **Instrumentation and process control:** error, accuracy and sensitivity; flow, pressure, temperature, level, pH, viscosity and composition measurement; feedback control and controller settings.

## Numerical checklist

- [ ] Keep a formula sheet with symbols, units, assumptions and sign conventions.
- [ ] For every balance, draw the process boundary and label all streams.
- [ ] Choose a basis and write the balance equation before substituting values.
- [ ] Practise syllabus-matched recycle, reaction and energy-balance problems.
- [ ] Distinguish batch, CSTR and PFR assumptions before using reactor equations.
- [ ] Sketch a feedback-control loop; identify set point, measured variable, controller, manipulated variable and disturbance.
- [ ] Check dimensional consistency and units in every final answer.

## Answer structure for a balance problem

1. Draw and label the process.
2. State the basis and assumptions.
3. Convert units and define variables.
4. Write the general balance.
5. Substitute values and solve.
6. Check dimensions, signs and physical reasonableness.

### Watch/practise in this order
Unit conversions and dimensional consistency → process variables and basis selection → non-reactive balances → recycle/bypass/purge → reactive and combustion balances → energy balances → reactor equations and process-control diagrams. Solve before watching the worked solution.
